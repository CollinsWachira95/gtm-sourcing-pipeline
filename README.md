## ICP Definition

**Target:** German Mittelstand companies with 50–500 employees that are
multi-entity (multiple legal entities or subsidiaries). Multi-entity is a
filter, not a signal — it defines basic fit.

**Signals that matter (in priority order):**

1. **PE-backed (fit)** — why: a PE owner typically pushes consolidated
   reporting and standardized finance data across its portfolio. The
   consolidation pain ARC solves is structurally present, and the owner has
   budget and urgency to fix it.

2. **Recent acquisition (timing)** — why: integrating a newly acquired entity
   creates immediate consolidation and reporting complexity. The buying
   window opens right after the deal closes.

3. **Hiring ERP roles (timing)** — why: hiring for SAP / Oracle / Dynamics /
   DATEV roles signals an active finance-system migration or expansion.
   Data is in flux, which is exactly when consolidation and reporting pain
   peaks.

**Priority philosophy:** I prioritize **fit over timing**. A company with the
right structure has the consolidation pain ARC solves; timing decides when to
call, not whether they are the right buyer.

**Why this matters for ARC:** ARC sells an AI-native Finance OS that connects
ERP systems and consolidates fragmented finance data for Mittelstand CFOs.
Multi-entity, PE-backed companies have the most painful consolidation and
reporting problems — and the budget to solve them. Targeting them means BDRs
spend calls on prospects whose structural pain matches what ARC actually fixes.
